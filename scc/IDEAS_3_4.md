# New ideas for Item 3 (global interior) and Item 4 (Christodoulou formulation)

## Item 3 — the global bifurcate Cauchy horizon

**Geometry.** Two-ended Kerr data. The interior is a "diamond" bounded below by H⁺_A ∪ H⁺_B and above by CH⁺_A ∪ CH⁺_B, which meet at the bifurcation sphere S_CH. In double null coordinates, CH⁺_A is {v = ∞} and CH⁺_B is {u = ∞}. Luk–Sbierski and Dafermos–Luk control the region u ≤ u_f near CH⁺_A, near i⁺_A, and symmetrically near CH⁺_B. What remains:
- (R1) a compact middle region (u, v bounded). This is handled by Cauchy stability, no idea needed.
- (R2) strips along CH⁺_A with u ∈ [u_f, ∞), i.e. approaching the corner S_CH. Symmetric strips along CH⁺_B.
- (R3) **the corner** u, v → ∞.

**Idea 3.1: the corner is a transversal interaction of two weak null singularities.**
- In R3 both CH⁺_A and CH⁺_B are weakly singular. α (∼ Ψ₀) is singular in v, from the H⁺_A tail blue-shifted. α̲ (∼ Ψ₄) is singular in u, from the H⁺_B tail blue-shifted.
- No estimate that includes α or α̲ can close. The Luk–Rodnianski framework for **interacting impulsive gravitational waves** (Luk–Rodnianski, Ann. PDE / Cambridge J. Math.) was built exactly for this. It uses the **renormalised Bianchi system** in (β, ρ̌, σ̌, β̲), with ρ̌ = ρ − ½χ̂·χ̲̂ and σ̌ = σ + ½χ̂∧χ̲̂, which never contains α or α̲. The Ricci coefficients χ̂ and χ̲̂ are allowed to be only L² in v (respectively u), not L∞.
- Dafermos–Luk already use Luk's renormalised norms (designed in his JAMS paper) for one singular direction.
- **Proposal:** run Dafermos–Luk's polynomially weighted blue-shift estimates simultaneously in u and v, with weights |u|^{q}|v|^{q}, using the doubly renormalised Luk–Rodnianski system.
- **Smallness:** the corner is "late" in both directions, so data on {u = U} ∪ {v = V} for large U and V are small in the doubly weighted norms. This comes from the decay along both H⁺_A and H⁺_B (Hintz) propagated by Dafermos–Luk along both families of strips.
- **Key structural check:** the only dangerous products are χ̂·χ̲̂ (L²_v × L²_u ⊂ L¹_{uv}) and their appearance in ρ̌ and σ̌. Luk–Rodnianski's null structure shows they enter only in integrable combinations. **This needs the Kerr background terms with a ≠ 0 (ζ, η ≠ 0 in the background) to be shown harmless in the corner. The candidate obstruction is frame-dragging: background η, η̲ are O(1), not small, so the background-times-singular terms η·α̲-type must be handled by the renormalisation.**
- **Instability on all of CH⁺_A, not just near i⁺:** Sbierski's linear criterion is local along CH⁺. Luk–Sbierski's nonlinear approximation (dynamical ψ₀ ≈ linear ψ₀ up to faster decay) has to be extended into R2. There the "u large" smallness is replaced by the corner estimates above. Expected to be perturbative.

**Assessment:** Item 3 needs a new combination (Dafermos–Luk with two-sided weights plus Luk–Rodnianski renormalisation in the Kerr background), not a new mechanism. The risk is concentrated in the background η-terms in the corner.

## Item 4 — Christodoulou's formulation (inextendibility with Γ ∈ L²_loc)

### Why Lipschitz inextendibility is not enough

At a weak null singularity in Luk's sense, the metric is C⁰, and the *transversal* Christoffel symbols χ̂ are **in L²_v**: only α, one derivative higher, is singular. So a weak null singularity driven only by Ψ₀ blue-shift may be **H¹-extendible**, and Christodoulou's formulation could then fail. Christodoulou chose L² Christoffels precisely because of **mass inflation**: in spherical symmetry the Hawking mass, which is *quadratic* in Christoffels, blows up, and that is incompatible with Γ ∈ L². So:

> **Item 4 = nonlinear mass inflation in vacuum without symmetry (4a) + a geometric lemma: mass inflation ⇒ no C⁰ ∩ H¹_loc extension (4b).**

### 4a — the mass-inflation mechanism in vacuum, and a new observation about oscillations

The Bianchi equation along e₄ is
 ∇₄ρ + (3/2) tr χ ρ = div β − ½ χ̲̂·α + (lower order),
so ρ (the "mass aspect", ∼ Re Ψ₂) is driven by **∫ χ̲̂·α dv**. Near CH⁺_A, α is non-integrable: it is the v-derivative of χ̂ ∈ L² \ L∞. So ρ blows up iff χ̲̂|_{CH⁺_A} ≠ 0 **and** the product does not cancel by oscillation.

- **χ̲̂ ≠ 0 at CH⁺_A:** in Kerr, χ̲̂ = 0 in the background. At the perturbed CH⁺_A it is generated by *back-scattering inside the black hole*, which is the s = −2 field. Linear: Gurriaran, *Generic linearised curvature singularity at the perturbed Kerr Cauchy horizon* (arXiv:2503.24114), proves generic s = −2 blow-up. Nonlinear: needed.
- **The oscillation problem (Ori 1999, the "oscillatory null singularity").** In Kerr, α and χ̲̂ oscillate in v like e^{i m(Ω_H − Ω_CH) v}, from frame dragging. Pointwise, χ̲̂·α oscillates, and ∫ χ̲̂·α dv may *not* diverge. This is why mass inflation in Kerr has been murky.
- **Idea 4.1: sphere averaging removes the oscillation.** Use the **sphere-averaged** mass aspect ∫_{S(u,v)} ρ, or the Hawking mass. Then the φ-integration pairs the azimuthal mode m of α with the mode m of χ̲̂ in the form \overline{χ̲̂_m}·α_m. The frame-dragging phases e^{±imΩv} **cancel exactly**, because both are dragged by the same Ω. What remains is a non-oscillating, v-blue-shifted integrand. Moreover, Luk–Sbierski already show the blow-up lives in the **finite-dimensional ℓ = 2 sector**. So the averaged driving term is a finite sum Σ_{|m|≤2} \overline{c̲_m}(u) c_m e^{2κ₋ v} v^{−q}(1 + o(1)), and **generic non-cancellation reduces to a finite-dimensional non-degeneracy condition**: a 5×5 sesquilinear form in the ℓ = 2 coefficients being non-zero. This is the kind of statement that genericity can supply.
- **Idea 4.2: χ̲̂ is generated *from* α.** Inside the black hole the s = −2 field at CH⁺_A is (up to background-determined linear maps) the back-scatter of the s = +2 field, a Teukolsky–Starobinsky-type relation along the interior. If that relation is *positive* on ℓ = 2, then the sesquilinear form in 4.1 is a **positive definite quadratic form in the ℓ = 2 data**, and mass inflation would hold for **all** data satisfying Luk–Sbierski's lower bound, not just generic ones. **Concrete computation:** the interior Teukolsky–Starobinsky constants for ℓ = 2 at the Cauchy-horizon frequencies, in Kerr, using exact Kerr data. This is finite and checkable.

### 4b — the geometric lemma: averaged Hawking-mass blow-up ⇒ no C⁰ ∩ H¹_loc extension

**Difficulty.** In a hypothetical extension, the double-null spheres of the original spacetime need not be regular in the extension's chart, and with Γ ∈ L² geodesics are not well defined. That kills Sbierski's Lipschitz method.

**Idea 4.3: a Fubini argument over a 2-parameter family of spheres.**
- The Hawking mass m_H(S) = (r/2)(1 + (1/16π)∫_S θθ̲) is boost-invariant and **quadratic in first derivatives of the metric** restricted to S.
- For a C⁰ ∩ H¹_loc metric, ∫∫ |θθ̲| over a smooth 2-parameter family of spheres filling a 4-dimensional region is ≤ C‖Γ‖²_{L²} (Fubini + Cauchy–Schwarz). So the **averaged** Hawking mass is finite for **chart-smooth** sphere families.
- **Gap:** our blow-up is for **double-null** spheres. We need them to be uniformly Lipschitz in any C⁰ ∩ H¹ extension chart, or at least to have the same averaged Hawking mass up to bounded error as nearby chart-smooth spheres.
- Proposed route: *Hawking mass is monotone along null directions under the dominant energy condition (vacuum).* That is the Raychaudhuri-based monotonicity used in Penrose-inequality arguments. Use monotonicity to transfer the blow-up from double-null spheres to **any** spacelike sphere lying in the causal future of them, within the original spacetime. Then pick chart-smooth spheres of the extension approaching the boundary point from inside. Their Hawking masses must then also blow up, contradicting finiteness by Fubini.
- **The monotonicity has a sign condition:** it needs the spheres to be untrapped or marginally trapped in the right direction. Near CH⁺ in Kerr the spheres are **trapped** (θ, θ̲ < 0). So the relevant monotonicity is the trapped-region version: the Hawking mass is non-decreasing along the future null directions **inside trapped regions**, exactly as in spherical symmetry, where m is monotone in the trapped region. **Check:** the precise non-spherical statement. The Hawking-mass variation along a null flow has a sign only up to terms |χ̂|², |ζ|² that have the *favourable* sign in the trapped region (Christodoulou, Bray–Hayward–Mars–Simon). This should hold, but the error terms involving ζ (torsion, which is nonzero in Kerr) need control.
