# New ideas for Item 3 (global interior) and Item 4 (Christodoulou formulation)

## Item 3 — the global bifurcate Cauchy horizon

**Geometry.** Two-ended Kerr data. The interior is a "diamond" bounded below by H⁺_A ∪ H⁺_B and above by CH⁺_A ∪ CH⁺_B, which meet at the bifurcation sphere S_CH. In double null coordinates, CH⁺_A is {v = ∞} and CH⁺_B is {u = ∞}. Luk–Sbierski and Dafermos–Luk control the region u ≤ u_f near CH⁺_A, near i⁺_A, and symmetrically near CH⁺_B. What remains:
- (R1) a compact middle region (u, v bounded). This is handled by Cauchy stability, no idea needed.
- (R2) strips along CH⁺_A with u ∈ [u_f, ∞), i.e. approaching the corner S_CH. Symmetric strips along CH⁺_B.
- (R3) **the corner** u, v → ∞.

**Idea 3.1: the corner is a transversal interaction of two weak null singularities.**
- In R3 both CH⁺_A and CH⁺_B are weakly singular. α (∼ Ψ₀) is singular in v, from the H⁺_A tail blue-shifted. α̲ (∼ Ψ₄) is singular in u, from the H⁺_B tail blue-shifted.
- No estimate that includes α or α̲ can close. The Luk–Rodnianski framework for **interacting impulsive gravitational waves** (Luk–Rodnianski, Ann. PDE / Cambridge J. Math.) was built exactly for this. It uses the **renormalised Bianchi system** in (β, ρ̌, σ̌, β̲), with ρ̌ = ρ − ½χ̂·χ̲̂ and σ̌ = σ + ½χ̂∧χ̲̂, which never contains α or α̲. The Ricci coefficients χ̂ and χ̲̂ are allowed to be only L² in v (respectively u), not L∞.
- Dafermos–Luk already use Luk's renormalised norms (designed in his JAMS paper) for one singular direction.
- **Proposal:** run Dafermos–Luk's polynomially weighted blue-shift estimates simultaneously in u and v, with weights |u|^{q}|v|^{q}, using the doubly renormalised Luk–Rodnianski system.
- **Smallness:** the corner is "late" in both directions, so data on {u = U} ∪ {v = V} for large U and V are small in the doubly weighted norms. This comes from the decay along both H⁺_A and H⁺_B (Hintz) propagated by Dafermos–Luk along both families of strips.
- **Key structural check:** the only dangerous products are χ̂·χ̲̂ (L²_v × L²_u ⊂ L¹_{uv}) and their appearance in ρ̌ and σ̌. Luk–Rodnianski's null structure shows they enter only in integrable combinations. **This needs the Kerr background terms with a ≠ 0 (ζ, η ≠ 0 in the background) to be shown harmless in the corner. The candidate obstruction is frame-dragging: background η, η̲ are O(1), not small, so the background-times-singular terms η·α̲-type must be handled by the renormalisation.**
- **Instability on all of CH⁺_A, not just near i⁺:** Sbierski's linear criterion is local along CH⁺. Luk–Sbierski's nonlinear approximation (dynamical ψ₀ ≈ linear ψ₀ up to faster decay) has to be extended into R2. There the "u large" smallness is replaced by the corner estimates above. Expected to be perturbative.

**Assessment:** Item 3 needs a new combination (Dafermos–Luk with two-sided weights plus Luk–Rodnianski renormalisation in the Kerr background), not a new mechanism. The risk is concentrated in the background η-terms in the corner.

## Item 4 — Christodoulou's formulation (inextendibility with Γ ∈ L²_loc)

### Why Lipschitz inextendibility is not enough

At a weak null singularity in Luk's sense, the metric is C⁰, and the *transversal* Christoffel symbols χ̂ are **in L²_v**: only α, one derivative higher, is singular. So a weak null singularity driven only by Ψ₀ blue-shift may be **H¹-extendible**, and Christodoulou's formulation could then fail. Christodoulou chose L² Christoffels precisely because of **mass inflation**: in spherical symmetry the Hawking mass, which is *quadratic* in Christoffels, blows up, and that is incompatible with Γ ∈ L². So:

> **Item 4 = nonlinear mass inflation in vacuum without symmetry (4a) + a geometric lemma: mass inflation ⇒ no C⁰ ∩ H¹_loc extension (4b).**

### 4a — the mass-inflation mechanism in vacuum, and a new observation about oscillations

The Bianchi equation along e₄ is
 ∇₄ρ + (3/2) tr χ ρ = div β − ½ χ̲̂·α + (lower order),
so ρ (the "mass aspect", ∼ Re Ψ₂) is driven by **∫ χ̲̂·α dv**. Near CH⁺_A, α is non-integrable: it is the v-derivative of χ̂ ∈ L² \ L∞. So ρ blows up iff χ̲̂|_{CH⁺_A} ≠ 0 **and** the product does not cancel by oscillation.

- **χ̲̂ ≠ 0 at CH⁺_A:** in Kerr, χ̲̂ = 0 in the background. At the perturbed CH⁺_A it is generated by *back-scattering inside the black hole*, which is the s = −2 field. Linear: Gurriaran, *Generic linearised curvature singularity at the perturbed Kerr Cauchy horizon* (arXiv:2503.24114), proves generic s = −2 blow-up. Nonlinear: needed.
- **The oscillation problem (Ori 1999, the "oscillatory null singularity").** In Kerr, α and χ̲̂ oscillate in v like e^{i m(Ω_H − Ω_CH) v}, from frame dragging. Pointwise, χ̲̂·α oscillates, and ∫ χ̲̂·α dv may *not* diverge. This is why mass inflation in Kerr has been murky.
- **Idea 4.1: sphere averaging removes the oscillation.** Use the **sphere-averaged** mass aspect ∫_{S(u,v)} ρ, or the Hawking mass. Then the φ-integration pairs the azimuthal mode m of α with the mode m of χ̲̂ in the form \overline{χ̲̂_m}·α_m. The frame-dragging phases e^{±imΩv} **cancel exactly**, because both are dragged by the same Ω. What remains is a non-oscillating, v-blue-shifted integrand. Moreover, Luk–Sbierski already show the blow-up lives in the **finite-dimensional ℓ = 2 sector**. So the averaged driving term is a finite sum Σ_{|m|≤2} \overline{c̲_m}(u) c_m e^{2κ₋ v} v^{−q}(1 + o(1)), and **generic non-cancellation reduces to a finite-dimensional non-degeneracy condition**: a 5×5 sesquilinear form in the ℓ = 2 coefficients being non-zero. This is the kind of statement that genericity can supply.
- **Idea 4.2: χ̲̂ is generated *from* α.** Inside the black hole the s = −2 field at CH⁺_A is (up to background-determined linear maps) the back-scatter of the s = +2 field, a Teukolsky–Starobinsky-type relation along the interior. If that relation is *positive* on ℓ = 2, then the sesquilinear form in 4.1 is a **positive definite quadratic form in the ℓ = 2 data**, and mass inflation would hold for **all** data satisfying Luk–Sbierski's lower bound, not just generic ones. **Concrete computation:** the interior Teukolsky–Starobinsky constants for ℓ = 2 at the Cauchy-horizon frequencies, in Kerr, using exact Kerr data. This is finite and checkable.

### 4b — the geometric lemma: averaged Hawking-mass blow-up ⇒ no C⁰ ∩ H¹_loc extension

**Difficulty.** In a hypothetical extension, the double-null spheres of the original spacetime need not be regular in the extension's chart, and with Γ ∈ L² geodesics are not well defined. That kills Sbierski's Lipschitz method.

**Idea 4.3: a Fubini argument over a 2-parameter family of spheres.**
- The Hawking mass m_H(S) = (r/2)(1 + (1/16π)∫_S θθ̲) is boost-invariant and **quadratic in first derivatives of the metric** restricted to S.
- For a C⁰ ∩ H¹_loc metric, ∫∫ |θθ̲| over a smooth 2-parameter family of spheres filling a 4-dimensional region is ≤ C‖Γ‖²_{L²} (Fubini + Cauchy–Schwarz). So the **averaged** Hawking mass is finite for **chart-smooth** sphere families.
- **Gap:** our blow-up is for **double-null** spheres. We need them to be uniformly Lipschitz in any C⁰ ∩ H¹ extension chart, or at least to have the same averaged Hawking mass up to bounded error as nearby chart-smooth spheres.
- Proposed route: *Hawking mass is monotone along null directions under the dominant energy condition (vacuum).* That is the Raychaudhuri-based monotonicity used in Penrose-inequality arguments. Use monotonicity to transfer the blow-up from double-null spheres to **any** spacelike sphere lying in the causal future of them, within the original spacetime. Then pick chart-smooth spheres of the extension approaching the boundary point from inside. Their Hawking masses must then also blow up, contradicting finiteness by Fubini.
- **The monotonicity has a sign condition:** it needs the spheres to be untrapped or marginally trapped in the right direction. Near CH⁺ in Kerr the spheres are **trapped** (θ, θ̲ < 0). So the relevant monotonicity is the trapped-region version: the Hawking mass is non-decreasing along the future null directions **inside trapped regions**, exactly as in spherical symmetry, where m is monotone in the trapped region. **Check:** the precise non-spherical statement. The Hawking-mass variation along a null flow has a sign only up to terms |χ̂|², |ζ|² that have the *favourable* sign in the trapped region (Christodoulou, Bray–Hayward–Mars–Simon). This should hold, but the error terms involving ζ (torsion, which is nonzero in Kerr) need control.

# Step 1 results (literature check): two corrections, one of them major

## Correction A: Idea 4.1 is refuted as stated, and it was not needed

Gurriaran (arXiv:2503.24114, eq. (1.7) and Thm 3.3; and his CMP 2025 paper on s = +2):
- ψ̂₊₂ ~ Δ⁻² u̲⁻⁷ Σ_{|m|≤2} A_m(r₋) Q_{m,2} **e^{2im r_mod}** Y^{+2}_{m,2} e^{imφ₋}, with r_mod ~ a log(r − r₋). This is the "violent oscillation".
- ψ̂₋₂ ~ u⁻⁸ Σ C_m Y^{−2}_{m,2} e^{imφ₋}. This is **regular** at CH⁺ and has **no** e^{2im r_mod} factor.

So the two fields do **not** carry the same frame-dragging phase. In the sphere-averaged product, pairing m with −m in the φ-integral still leaves the factor e^{2im r_mod}, so averaging does **not** remove the oscillation. Idea 4.1 is false.

It is also unnecessary. The oscillation is log-periodic in Δ, i.e. it sits on top of an exponentially growing amplitude e^{2κ₋v}. Integrals ∫ e^{(2κ₋ + iω_m)v} dv blow up in modulus whatever the phase, and a finite sum of distinct frequencies with nonzero coefficients cannot be o(amplitude). So the oscillation does not prevent blow-up. (Gurriaran also notes A_m(r₋) ≠ 0 only for m = ±1, ±2.)

## Correction B (major): Christodoulou's formulation does NOT require mass inflation

From Dafermos–Luk (arXiv:1710.01722, §1.4 and §1.5.3, Luk's local weights):
- At a weak null singularity, in the regular coordinate U, the shear behaves like
 χ̂ ~ (−U)⁻¹ |log(−U)|^{−1−2δ}.
 This is in L¹ but **not in L²**.
- So the **Christoffel symbols fail to be L² already at the level of χ̂**, i.e. directly from the s = +2 (α / Ψ₀) blue-shift. No product of two singular fields and no Hawking-mass blow-up is involved.
- DL state this explicitly: for the continuous extensions in Luk's construction, the Christoffel symbols fail to be square integrable, and conjecturally this holds for **any** continuous extension. That conjecture is exactly what Christodoulou's formulation needs.

**My earlier claim "χ̂ ∈ L²_v at a weak null singularity" was wrong:** the weighted quantity f(U)χ̂ is L², not χ̂ itself. Consequently:
- Item **F2 (nonlinear mass inflation) is not on the critical path** for Christodoulou's formulation near Kerr. It is a separate, stronger statement about Hawking-mass blow-up.
- There is also a structural reason mass inflation is harder in vacuum. The Hawking mass is governed by the **renormalised** quantities ρ̌ = ρ − ½χ̂·χ̲̂ (equivalently the Gauss curvature K), and ∇₄ trχ̲ involves ρ̌, not α. So the Hawking mass does not see α directly, and Idea 4.2 (Teukolsky–Starobinsky positivity for mass inflation) is moot for Item 4.

## Item 4, restated correctly

> **(F1′) Low-regularity inextendibility.** Luk–Sbierski / Gurriaran give the following. In the Dafermos–Luk chart (u, U_CH, θ), the metric is C⁰ up to CH⁺ and the Christoffel symbol χ̂ satisfies a weighted lower bound that forces χ̂ ∉ L²_loc. **Show that no C⁰ extension (M̃, g̃) has Γ̃ ∈ L²_loc near any point of ι(CH⁺).**

The only obstruction is **non-uniqueness of the differentiable structure at the boundary**. Uniqueness of continuous extensions across weak null singularities is known to fail. Sbierski proved uniqueness at the Lipschitz level and deduced Lipschitz inextendibility from it. We need the Christoffel-L² level.

## New ideas for F1′

**Idea F-a: Regularity of the optical function in any extension (eikonal + Raychaudhuri).**
- Let u be the DL optical function, so that the u = const hypersurfaces are null, with generators L = −∇u. In an extension chart near p, u is defined only on ι(M).
- **Step 1:** show that u extends continuously to a neighbourhood of p in M̃ with ∇u ∈ C⁰. The level sets are achronal null hypersurfaces in a C⁰ spacetime, which are Lipschitz hypersurfaces by C⁰ causality theory (Chruściel–Grant). Then improve to C¹ using that their generators are limits of smooth null geodesics, i.e. via the known C^{1,1} regularity of null hypersurfaces with bounded expansion. **This is the delicate point, since here the expansion is unbounded.**
- **Step 2:** in the extension chart, ∇²u = ∂²u − Γ̃·∂u. Its restriction to the spheres is χ, and χ̂ ∉ L². If Γ̃ ∈ L² and ∂u ∈ L∞, then ∂²u ∉ L² near p.
- **Step 3: contradiction from the eikonal equation.** Differentiate g̃^{ab}∂_a u ∂_b u = 0. With g̃ ∈ C⁰ ∩ H¹ this gives g̃^{ab}∂_a u ∂_b∂_c u = −½(∂_c g̃^{ab})∂_a u ∂_b u ∈ L². So **the L-directional second derivatives of u are L²**. The **angular** ones (χ̂) are not. They are linked by the Riccati/Raychaudhuri equation along L,
 L(χ̂) = −trχ χ̂ − α (schematically),
 so the obstruction is the size of α as a distribution. In a Γ̃ ∈ L² chart, α = (∂Γ̃ + Γ̃Γ̃)-components lies in H⁻¹ + L¹, while our α is a blue-shifted, non-H⁻¹ object along the generators. **Precise test:** does Luk–Sbierski's weighted L² lower bound on α (ℓ = 2 modes) imply α ∉ H⁻¹_loc + L¹_loc, uniformly in the class of C¹ coordinate changes that fix the generators? If yes, this gives a chart-independent obstruction at the Christoffel-L² level.

**Idea F-b: Lorentzian-length / synthetic route (backup).**
A C⁰ metric defines a Lorentzian length space (Kunzinger–Sämann), and Γ ∈ L² with *weak* Ric = 0 should give a synthetic timelike-Ricci bound (TCD(0, 4)-type, Cavalletti–Mondino). That would be violated by the unbounded tidal focusing at the weak null singularity (θ → −∞ along generators while area stays bounded). This needs the extension to be a weak solution, which Christodoulou's formulation does **not** assume. So it proves a weaker variant: inextendibility **as a weak solution of the vacuum equations**. This is still meaningful (it is Christodoulou's stated motivation), and plausibly more tractable.

# Working F-a: what goes through and where it stops

**Setup.** M is the Dafermos–Luk (DL) interior near CH⁺, with regular DL coordinates (u, U, θ), CH⁺ = {U = 0}, and frame vector V := ∂_U (null, tangent to the outgoing generators). DL give: the metric is C⁰ up to U = 0, Ω is continuous and nonzero, and the volume density in (U, u, θ) is continuous and positive. Luk–Sbierski / Gurriaran give: the shear χ̂ of V is ∉ L²(dU du dθ), with χ̂ ~ U⁻¹|log U|^{−1−2δ}, which is L¹ but not L².

Suppose (M̃, g̃) is an extension, x a chart near p ∈ ι(CH⁺), g̃ ∈ C⁰, Γ̃ ∈ L²_loc. Let Φ: (U, u, θ) ↦ x be the transition map. It is smooth for U < 0 and continuous up to U = 0.

## Lemma F1 (proved in sketch): in any such extension, the generator field and the Jacobi fields are bounded, uniformly up to the Cauchy horizon, along a.e. generator

1. **Fubini is available.** Both the DL volume density (in (U, u, θ)) and the chart density (in x) are continuous and positive, so Φ pushes Lebesgue measure to a measure with bounded density. Hence ∫∫ (∫_{U₀}^0 |Γ̃(Φ(U, u, θ))|² dU) du dθ ≲ ‖Γ̃‖²_{L²}. So for **a.e.** generator, Γ̃ ∘ Φ ∈ L²(dU) ⊂ L¹(dU) on [U₀, 0).
2. **Bounded tangents.** Fix a chart-smooth timelike field T and let F := −g̃(V, T) > 0. The geodesic equation (with V affine up to the continuous lapse) gives dV^a/dU = −Γ̃^a_{bc}V^bV^c + (continuous)·V. Hence |d(1/F)/dU| ≲ |Γ̃∘Φ| + 1. Choose U₀ close to 0 so that the L¹ tail of Γ̃∘Φ is small. Then F is bounded above and below on [U₀, 0). Since g̃ is C⁰, null vectors with F ~ 1 have bounded chart components, so V^a is **bounded, and bounded away from 0, up to CH⁺**.
3. **Bounded Jacobi fields.** Let Y := ∂_θΦ. Then dY/dU = ∂_θ V = χ(Y) + Γ̃(Y, V)-terms. Here χ = χ̂ + ½trχ is **L¹ in U** (the key fact: a weak null singularity is L¹ but not L² in the shear). Gronwall in both directions gives **Y bounded above and below up to CH⁺**.

⇒ In any C⁰ ∩ (Γ̃ ∈ L²) extension, Φ is **bi-Lipschitz up to CH⁺ along a.e. generator**: ∂Φ ∈ L∞ with bounded inverse. *(This is the Christoffel-L² analogue of the step that Sbierski needs geodesics in a Lipschitz extension for. Here Fubini plus the L¹ (not L²) integrability of the shear replaces that.)*

## Reduction: what an extension would need to do

Christoffel symbols transform as Γ_DL = (∂Φ)⁻¹[∂²Φ + Γ̃(∂Φ, ∂Φ)]. With ∂Φ bounded (Lemma F1) and Γ̃ ∈ L²:

 **Γ_DL ∈ (∂Φ)⁻¹∂²Φ + L²**, i.e. **the singular part of Γ_DL (the shear χ̂ ~ U⁻¹log^{−1−2δ}) must be pure gauge modulo L².**

A pure-gauge connection (∂Φ)⁻¹∂²Φ is **flat**. The singular shear, on the other hand, carries curvature: α = ∂_Uχ̂ + … is a tensor, and Luk–Sbierski give a lower bound on it. So the extension exists only if the singular curvature α can be produced by the L² remainder, i.e. **α ∈ ∂(L²) + L²·L² in DL coordinates, tested against the bounded (but only W^{1,1}) frame fields V and Y.**

## Where it stops: a precise remaining question

The natural test is to pair α(Y, Y) with a cutoff φ and integrate by parts. This produces terms ∫ Γ̃ ∂(φ V Y V Y). Here ∂Y ∈ L¹ \ L² (Lemma F1 gives only boundedness of Y), so Γ̃ ∈ L² times ∂Y ∈ L¹ is **not** controlled. Equivalently, the H⁻¹ + L¹ test of my previous note fails at the **multiplier step**: L∞ ∩ W^{1,1} functions do not multiply H⁻¹.

> **(Q) Compensated-compactness question.** Let V and Y be bounded vector fields with ∂_U Y = χ̂ Y + (L²), where χ̂ ≥ c U⁻¹|log U|^{−1−2δ} on a set of positive measure (in an ℓ = 2 averaged sense). Is
>  sup_φ |∫ α(Y, Y) φ| / (‖Γ̃‖_{L²}‖∂φ‖ + …) = ∞
> forced?

There is reason to expect structure. The bad product Γ̃·∂_U Y has ∂_U Y **along the generator**, while Γ̃'s relevant components are tied to the null structure. That suggests a **div–curl / null-form** cancellation: the bad derivative falls in the null direction, where Γ̃'s component V-V-contracted is better, being ∂F-controlled by step 2. This is the next thing to test.

## Status

- **Lemma F1 is new and robust.** It says Christoffel-L² extensions are automatically bi-Lipschitz on the DL structure, a.e. along generators.
- F-a now hinges on one bilinear estimate, **(Q)**: the "pure gauge modulo L²" obstruction.
- If (Q) fails, the singular shear could be gauged away in L². That would mean **Christodoulou's formulation is false near Kerr**, an equally important outcome. So (Q) is the decisive question either way.
